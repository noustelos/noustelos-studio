Acquisition

Own the verb

Noustelos Studio · 3 August 2026 · 5 min read

Most domains are labels. A few are instructions. This one is a sentence people already say out loud before they ever type it — and that is the whole argument.

There is a category of domain name that doesn’t describe a company so much as describe an action. You don’t have to teach anyone what to do with it. Nobody needs a tagline explaining ask Singapore. The instruction is the name, the name is the product, and the gap between what a visitor wants and what they type is zero.

That is rare, and it is the reason this domain exists as a product rather than a parked page.

Why “ask” and why now

For twenty years, finding something in an unfamiliar city meant search: type keywords, receive a list, open ten tabs, decide. That interface is being replaced by a conversational one — you state your situation in a sentence and get an answer back. Not a list. An answer.

Names built for the list era describe inventory: hotels-singapore, bestsingaporerestaurants. Names built for the answer era describe the act of asking. When the interface changes, the naming convention changes with it, and the good names in the new convention are claimed exactly once.

“Ask” is short, imperative, universally understood by non-native speakers, and reads as a promise rather than a category. Paired with a destination, it becomes a brand that requires no explanation in any market.

The test we use: can a visitor say the domain out loud to a friend, in a noisy bar, and have them type it correctly the next morning? Very few names pass. This one does.

What actually comes with it

This is not a parked name with a logo mocked up over it. The concierge on the front page is live: you can open it right now, ask a question in any language, and read a real streamed answer. What transfers to a buyer:

  • The domain — asksingapore.ai, transferred outright.
  • The working concierge — the page and its serverless chat endpoint, deployed and running, not a video or a mockup.
  • The full source — one HTML page, one serverless function, no framework, no build step, no dependency tree to inherit. A competent developer can read the entire thing in an afternoon and change anything in it.
  • The guardrail work — the venue rules and hand-checked verified list that stop the model inventing places, described in detail in the how-it-works note. This is the part that took the longest and is the least obvious from the outside.
  • The brand system — identity, typography, the design language, the social card.

Equally, here is what does not come with it, stated plainly so nobody discovers it after a call: there is no traffic history to speak of, no user base, no revenue, no booking integrations, no mobile app. It is a domain and a working proof of concept. Anyone telling you a demo is a business is selling you something.

The paths a buyer can take

The concierge is deliberately built so the obvious revenue mechanics slot in without rearchitecting anything. Four paths, none of them speculative technology:

  • Affiliate commissions — tours, transfers, experiences and stays, attributed on outbound clicks. This is running on the network flagship, asksantorini.ai, which already operates a working affiliate attribution system.
  • Featured listings — local operators paying for presence in the concierge’s recommendations, within the verified-list model that already governs which places can be named.
  • Booking integration — end-to-end reservations on commission or a flat fee, once a real inventory partner is connected.
  • Sponsored answers — premium placement, clearly labelled as such. The labelling is not optional; it is the only version of this that survives contact with users.

We are not going to publish a revenue projection for any of them. Numbers invented on a landing page are the same failure mode as a chatbot inventing a restaurant — confident, specific, and unearned.

Who this actually fits

The buyers this makes obvious sense for are the ones who already have the missing half:

  • A tourism or hospitality group with inventory but no conversational front door.
  • A travel booking platform wanting a destination-specific acquisition channel that doesn’t compete with its own brand.
  • An AI or agent startup targeting Southeast Asia that needs a name people can remember and spell.
  • A publisher or media brand with Singapore audience and content, and no product to point it at.
  • A domain investor building a portfolio in the conversational-AI naming convention while the good names are still available.

Part of a network, not a one-off

asksingapore.ai belongs to a set built on the same architecture: asksantorini.ai, asksydney.ai, askaustralia.ai, asknewyork.ai and askmykonos.ai.

That matters for two reasons. It shows the model is repeatable rather than a one-off experiment — the same stack, re-pointed at a different city, with a different verified list. And it means a buyer thinking bigger than one destination has a conversation available beyond a single name.

Why there is no price on this page

Because we don’t know what it is worth to you, and pretending otherwise helps neither of us.

A number on a page does one of two things. Set too high, it ends conversations that should have happened. Set too low, it caps an asset in front of the one buyer for whom it would have been worth considerably more. The same domain is genuinely worth different amounts to a hotel group with ten thousand Singapore room-nights to fill and to an investor holding a name for three years.

So: no asking price, no valuation framework, no anchoring. Tell us what asksingapore.ai is worth in your hands and what you would do with it. Serious offers get a serious answer, quickly.
